## 1. Target Market Overview

Cooperative finance is built on democratic member control, member economic participation, and concern for community. Digital Coop Bank brings the Mongolian **savings and credit cooperative (SCC)** model to a purely digital delivery channel. The honest niche is **the digitized SCC cooperative** — member ownership, democratic governance, and disciplined saving — **not** "a neobank for the underbanked." Mongolia has no underbanked gap to close, and its incumbents are already digital-first at a level a new entrant cannot beat on convenience.

### 1.1 The Mongolian market reality (verified anchors)

These are the facts that set the thesis. All are from `CLAUDE.md`'s verified anchor set.

| Fact | Figure | What it means for the product |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Account ownership** | **98.3%** (World Bank Findex 2024); **rural 98.8% > urban** | There is **no underbanked segment** to serve. "Access to a bank account" is not a value proposition here. |
| **Borrowing** | **68.3%** — roughly twice the peer average | The market is **over-credited**. Expanding consumer credit is not a durable differentiator, and `CLAUDE.md` records that lending should be deferred. |
| **Saving** | **47.9%** | The market is **under-saved**. A savings-first, ownership-linked proposition is where an honest gap exists. |
| **Khan Bank scale** | **2.9m customers (≈82% of the population)**, **99.4% of transactions digital**, a **58-mini-app super-app** | "Neobank parity" is table stakes already delivered by the dominant incumbent — it is **not** a differentiator. |
| **SCC sector size** | **172 cooperatives / 78,608 members / MNT 390bn total** (FRC-regulated) | The entire existing SCC member base is **78,608** — smaller than the baseline's 100,000-member KPI. The addressable market must be sized against this reality, not against a neobank TAM. |

### 1.2 The honest niche

The defensible position is **not** convenience, access, or credit — incumbents win all three. It is the one thing no Mongolian commercial bank or super-app structurally offers: **member ownership**. A digital SCC gives a member a share, a vote, and a claim on the surplus (patronage), plus a savings-first orientation that fits an under-saved market. That is the product thesis this document defends.

Two constraints bound the segment and are surfaced honestly rather than assumed away:

* **Common bond (SCC Law Art. 19.3 — a `CLAUDE.md` blocking question).** SCC members must share an employer, association, or aimag/district. A nationwide, open-membership app may not be a lawful SCC. Until a Mongolian lawyer resolves this, the realistic target is **bounded, common-bond communities** (an employer, a professional association, a district), not the general public.
* **Savings are not insured.** SCC deposits are **not** covered by deposit insurance (the Law on Insurance of Bank Deposits covers banks only). This must be disclosed plainly and never marketed as protected or guaranteed. It is a genuine competitive disadvantage versus a licensed bank and is treated as such.

### 1.3 Market drivers relevant to Mongolia

1. **A digital-native population served by digital-first incumbents.** Near-universal account ownership and Khan Bank's ≈99.4%-digital transaction base mean members already expect mobile-first service. This is a **cost of entry**, not an opportunity.
2. **An under-saved market (47.9%).** The structural opening is helping members save and share in a surplus they own — not lending them more.
3. **An existing but fragmented SCC sector (172 cooperatives).** Legacy SCCs run on branch/paper processes; digitizing member ownership, governance, and savings is the modernization opportunity — within the legal bounds above.
4. **Demand for financial democracy / ownership.** No commercial bank or super-app gives members a vote or a patronage claim; the cooperative structure is the only lever an incumbent cannot copy without changing what it is.

## 2. Competitor & Analogous-Platform Profiles

The competitive set is **Mongolian**. Only Khan Bank carries `CLAUDE.md`-verified figures; every other local player is described **by category, qualitatively**, with any specific number, share, or feature claim marked `[UNVERIFIED]` rather than invented. International cooperatives appear **only** as explicitly-labelled analogues, never as local competitors.

### 2.1 Khan Bank — dominant universal bank & super-app (Mongolia) — *verified*

* **Positioning:** The dominant retail bank in Mongolia and the reference point for digital financial services. Verified: **2.9m customers (≈82% of the population)**, **99.4% of transactions digital**, and a **58-mini-app super-app** (`CLAUDE.md`).
* **Why it matters:** Khan Bank has already delivered the "digital-first, everything-in-one-app" experience at national scale. Any pitch built on onboarding speed, payment convenience, or app polish competes directly with this and loses.
* **What it does not offer (the opening):** It is a shareholder-owned commercial bank. Customers hold **no membership share, no vote, and no patronage claim** on the bank's surplus. Member ownership is the axis on which a cooperative differentiates; Khan Bank does not occupy it.
* **Note:** Only the four figures above are asserted as fact. Any further Khan Bank product, pricing, or roadmap detail is **`[UNVERIFIED]`** and not relied upon here.

### 2.2 Other large commercial banks (Mongolia) — *category, qualitative*

Mongolia's banking market includes several other large commercial banks — for example **Golomt Bank, Trade and Development Bank (TDB), State Bank, XacBank, and M Bank**. These are named as **the incumbent commercial-bank category**; **all specifics — customer counts, market share, digital-maturity, product features, cooperative history — are `[UNVERIFIED]` (not researched)** and no figures are asserted for them. As a category they share Khan Bank's structural gap: they are shareholder- or state-owned banks that do not offer member ownership, a member vote, or a patronage dividend.

### 2.3 The SCC sector — the closest structural peers (Mongolia) — *verified totals, qualitative detail*

* **Positioning:** Mongolia's existing **savings and credit cooperatives (SCCs)**, regulated by the **FRC (Financial Regulatory Commission)**. Verified sector totals: **172 cooperatives / 78,608 members / MNT 390bn** (`CLAUDE.md`).
* **Why it matters:** These are the true peers — same legal form, same member-ownership model, same common-bond constraint. They are the group Digital Coop Bank would modernize or compete within, not the banks.
* **Qualitative read (specifics `[UNVERIFIED]`):** the sector is **small and fragmented** (172 cooperatives averaging a few hundred members each), and legacy SCCs are understood to run largely on branch/paper processes. Any claim about a specific cooperative's size, technology, or performance is **`[UNVERIFIED]`** and not asserted.
* **Honest tension:** the entire sector is **78,608 members**. The baseline's 100,000-member KPI exceeds it — see §5.

### 2.4 E-money & payment-service providers (Mongolia) — *category only, deliberately unnamed*

Bank of Mongolia licenses **e-money issuers and payment-service providers** as a category. This document **does not name individual licensees**: a fabricated Mongolian e-money licensee list was previously retracted, and this rewrite will not repeat that error. The existence of the category is relevant (it is where fast digital payments and wallets sit, and where any card/round-up ambition would require licensing the SCC does not hold), but **the roster, market shares, and feature sets are `[UNVERIFIED]` (not researched)**. Per `CLAUDE.md`, SCCs have no payment-service power and none appears on Bank of Mongolia's PSP register, so this category is more a **regulatory boundary** than a head-to-head competitor.

### 2.5 International cooperative & governance analogues — *not local competitors*

The following are retained **only as international reference models** — evidence that digital cooperative governance and member-ownership mechanics work elsewhere. They are **not** competitors in Mongolia and no Mongolian market claim rests on them.

* **Desjardins Group (Canada) — analogue.** The leading North American cooperative financial group; demonstrates that members will use **digitized member returns and digital AGM voting** when offered. Cited as a model for cooperative governance, not as a market participant here.
* **Coop Pank (Estonia) — analogue.** A bank with cooperative roots leveraging strong national digital identity for near-instant onboarding; a model for **state-digital-ID-driven onboarding** (in Mongolia the equivalent is ХУР/XYP state-register lookup, per `CLAUDE.md`), not a local competitor.
* **Aspiration (US, defunct) — cautionary analogue.** Marketed "clean money" and drove strong ethical acquisition, but **collapsed in 2025** amid financial and legal trouble. The lesson carried forward: ethical/impact claims must sit on a **structurally honest, transparent** economic model. Not a going concern and not a competitor.
* **Web3 governance platforms (e.g. DAO frameworks, community-matched grant tooling) — analogue.** Prove that digital proposal voting, transparent treasuries, and programmatic distribution work at scale; their crypto-volatility and lack of regulatory fit define why the mechanics belong **inside a regulated fiat cooperative** instead. Illustrative only.

## 3. Common Industry Capabilities (Competitive Baseline)

In Mongolia these are **table stakes already delivered by incumbents** (Khan Bank's super-app being the benchmark) — matching them is the cost of entry, **not** a source of advantage. They are listed so the differentiators in §4 can be distinguished from mere parity.

1. **Frictionless digital onboarding & eKYC.** Automated identity verification and instant provisioning; onboarding in minutes. For a cooperative this additionally requires **common-bond / eligibility validation** and **digital purchase of the initial membership share** to activate voting rights. (Note: biometric eKYC is a `CLAUDE.md` blocking question; the compliant fallback is **ХУР/XYP state-register lookup**.)
2. **Core deposit accounts with a dual structure.** Separation of withdrawable savings/transaction accounts from non-withdrawable membership **share capital** (equity, dividend-bearing), plus goal-based savings pots. Amounts are denominated in **MNT** (minor units, per the money non-negotiable).
3. **Payments.** Instant internal member-to-member transfers and integration with **Mongolia's domestic rails — RTGS (Банксүлжээ / Banksuljee), ACH+ for lower-value clearing, and NETC for cards.** *(Cards for an SCC are entity-gated / likely unlawful per `CLAUDE.md` — EP-5; shown here as market context, not committed scope.)*
4. **Lending (market context only).** Incumbents offer full digital lending. For Digital Coop Bank this is **deferred** (`CLAUDE.md`: the market is over-credited at 68.3%, the SCC constraint set is heaviest, and pricing is unfavourable). Listed for completeness, not as MVP capability.
5. **Dividend / surplus distribution.** Tracking cooperative surplus, calculating per-member entitlements, and automated distribution with statements — genuinely cooperative and carried into §4.
6. **Baseline governance tooling.** Secure remote one-member-one-vote balloting, access to financial reports, and notifications for major decisions. (Whether an e-vote counts toward quorum is a `CLAUDE.md` blocking question.)
7. **Security & compliance, with honest disclosure.** MFA/biometrics, encryption, AML monitoring — **and a plain statement that member savings are NOT deposit-insured** (SCC deposits are not covered; never describe them as insured, protected, or guaranteed). Regulators: **FRC** for the cooperative, **Bank of Mongolia** for any banking/payment/e-money activity.

## 4. Strategic Differentiators for Digital Coop Bank

Re-anchored to Mongolia: the cooperative **structure** — member ownership, governance, and a savings-first, patronage-linked model — is the only axis on which a new entrant can beat digital-first incumbents, because it is the one thing they cannot copy without ceasing to be shareholder banks. The "neobank parity" and "underbanked" claims of the prior draft are dropped: parity is table stakes here, and there is no underbanked gap. Five differentiators (IDs preserved):

### 4.1 Embedded, Accessible Democratic Governance (with Proxy Delegation)

Governance moves from paper AGMs into the daily app: members draft proposals, discuss them in-app, and vote one-member-one-vote from their dashboard with full audit trails, plus revocable **Proxy Delegation** per proposal category. This is the clearest structural gap versus every Mongolian bank and super-app, none of which give a customer a vote. Target participation of 40%+ is a KPI target, not a claim; low single-digit turnout is the general cooperative-AGM baseline `[UNVERIFIED]` for Mongolia specifically. Dependency: the e-vote-quorum blocking question (`CLAUDE.md`) governs whether digital governance is legally effective.

### 4.2 Loan Circles — community-vouched, peer-guaranteed credit (bounded, deferred)

A digital take on mutual-guarantee / ROSCA traditions: a member invites a few fellow members to vouch for a loan, lowering rate and default risk through social accountability. **Framed honestly:** the market is already **over-credited (68.3%)** and `CLAUDE.md` defers lending, so this is **not** a volume-growth play and is entity-gated. It is retained as a differentiating *concept* — member-owned, socially-accountable credit as an alternative to high-cost borrowing — pending the legal/entity decision, not as MVP scope.

### 4.3 Integrated Community Funding Hub with Surplus Matching

A crowdfunding board inside the app: members and registered local organizations pitch community projects; members back them from savings and vote to allocate cooperative **Community Grant** funds, with a fixed share of annual surplus matching approved projects. This ties member ownership to visible local impact — something a shareholder bank's CSR budget does not replicate. All amounts in **MNT**.

### 4.4 Real-Time Patronage Dividend Engine & Tracker (savings-first)

Rather than an opaque annual payout, members see a live estimate of their projected **patronage dividend**, driven by savings balances, activity, and governance participation, with automated pro-rata allocation, payout, and tax-statement generation. This directly serves the **under-saved (47.9%)** thesis: it rewards saving with visible, owned surplus. (Estimates must be labelled "estimated"; tax treatment of SCC distributions is a `CLAUDE.md` blocking question — potential dividend-style 10% withholding.)

### 4.5 Transparent Capital Ledger & Impact Reporting

A real-time, visual account of where the cooperative's collective funds are deployed (local lending, community grants, liquidity reserves) plus per-member impact reporting. This is the verifiable transparency that member ownership makes credible and that a shareholder incumbent has no structural reason to provide — and, per the Aspiration lesson (§2.5), it must be genuinely honest rather than marketed.

## 5. Positioning Summary

Digital Coop Bank's honest position in Mongolia is a **digitized savings-and-credit cooperative**: the **member ownership, democratic governance, and patronage surplus** of an SCC, delivered on modern mobile infrastructure, in **MNT**, for a **specific common-bond community**. It does **not** win on access (98.3% already banked), on convenience (Khan Bank's super-app already sets that bar), or on credit (the market borrows at 68.3% and lending is deferred). It wins — if it wins — on the one thing incumbents structurally cannot offer: **a share, a vote, and a claim on the surplus**, paired with a **savings-first** orientation aimed at the under-saved 47.9%.

Two honest bounds define the opportunity and must not be papered over:

* **The common-bond question may bound the entire addressable market.** SCC Law Art. 19.3 requires a shared employer, association, or aimag/district; a nationwide open-membership app may not be a lawful SCC (`CLAUDE.md` blocking question). Until a Mongolian lawyer resolves it, the realistic market is one or more **bounded communities**, not the general public.
* **The sector is small, and the KPI exceeds it.** The **entire** Mongolian SCC sector is **78,608 members** across 172 cooperatives. The baseline's **100,000-member KPI exceeds the whole sector** — an unresolved tension that either requires creating net-new cooperative membership at national scale or revising the target down. It is surfaced here rather than assumed away.

International references (Desjardins, Coop Pank, Web3 governance) show the cooperative-governance mechanics work; Aspiration warns they must be structurally honest. None is a Mongolian competitor. The defensible claim is narrow and true: **no Mongolian incumbent combines member ownership, digital democratic governance, and a savings-first patronage model in one regulated cooperative app.**
